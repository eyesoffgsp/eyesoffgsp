Spartanburg County Sheriff

Request #1: All contracts, licenses, MOUs, MOAs, data agreements, letters of intent, State of Work documents, Scope of Work documents, and other agreement documents with Flock Safety or Flock Group that were active any time between 1/1/2020 and 10/30/2025.

Results: View the PDF online at MuckRock.com

Overview:

  • Falcon Flex portable LPR cameras qty 2 and software access for $7,000 annually. - pdf page 15
  • Contract effective on 02/22/2024 for 2 years, auto-renews for 2 more years, requires 30 day non-renewal notice. - pages 1 and 28
  • Data retention period for Spartanburg County is 30 days. Flock can collect the data, meaning copy it to another location and retain it indefinitely.- page 15 and 25
  • Flock has the right to use our (anonymized) data to train their AI. - page 25
  • Flock is not liable for any acts by county staff. If someone wins a lawsuit related to the misuse of the Flock system, taxpayers will foot the bill. - pages 3 and 22
  • Customers own the data, and Flock has a license to use it. Flock cannot sell the data. - page 24
  • Flock can disclose our data to other agencies or third parties. This brings into question the meaning of the county "owning" the data. If the county doesn't have full control of the data, then do they really own it? - page 27, page 4 sec. 6.10
  • Flock can push updates to software or devices, with no language restricting the addition of new features. - page 22
  • Flock allows non-sworn county employees to have Flock accounts. - page 24
  • If Flock devices are damaged, taxpayers will foot the bill for replacement. - page 29

Request #2: 1. Organization Audit in Flock. The report should include data logged from the period of June 1, 2025 to November 30, 2025. Per Flock's documentation, the Organization Audit is available within the Insights tab and is defined as searches done within the agency. The report should include all selectable columns. 2. Network Audit in Flock. The report should include data logged from the period of June 1, 2025 to November 30, 2025. Per Flock's documentation, the Network Audit is available within the Insights tab and is defined as searches of the organization's Flock network by any agency in the Flock System. The report should include all selectable columns.

Results: Denied due to no responsive documents. See it on MuckRock.com

What this means:

  • The Sheriff's office has failed to be transparent in how the Flock system is currently being used by county employees and outside agencies with access to county cameras.
  • Their response implies that they have no visibility themselves into how the Flock system is currently being used by outside agencies.

An agency needs to be able to see who is accessing their cameras. The officer name, license plate, case #, and filters are automatically redacted. That doesn't leave very much information for the agency to understand how their cameras are being used. And unless they're using a powerful analytics tool, 1 million lines per month is too many to sift through and identify potential misuse. There's a significant amount of trust being placed in the agencies that they share with, and they're sharing with several thousand agencies. An agency can't possibly know the leadership at all those other agencies well enough to trust that their camera's aren't being misused against the community that they serve.
